10  Waves in Stratified Atmospheres

10.1 Waves in Isothermal Atmospheres

Consider a stratified, static, isothermal atmosphere. We assume constant acceleration \(g\) in the negative \(z\) direction. The hydrostatic equilibrium equations are \[ \partial_z p_0 = - \rho_0 g, \qquad p = a^2 \rho, \tag{10.1}\] where \(a = \sqrt{\frac{kT}{\mu}}\) is the isothermal sound speed.

Combining these two we find \[ \partial_z \rho_0 = - \frac{g}{a ^2} \rho_0 \Longrightarrow \rho_0 = \tilde{\rho} e^{- z / H} . \tag{10.2}\] \(H = a^2 / g\) is called the scale-high of the atmosphere and has units of a length.

We want to study the propagation of waves in this atmosphere. The continuity equation still gives \[ \delta \rho = - \rho_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}. \tag{10.3}\] and the velocity perturbation is \[ \mathbf{v}' = \delta \mathbf{v}- (\boldsymbol{\xi} \cdot \nabla) \mathbf{v}_0 = \delta \mathbf{v}= \frac{\mathrm{d} \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\mathrm{d} t} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{v}_0 \cdot \nabla) \boldsymbol{\xi}= \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t} . \tag{10.4}\] The sound speed is given by \[ c_s^2 = \left( \frac{\partial p}{\partial \rho} \right)_s = \Gamma a^2 . \tag{10.5}\] Which implies that \(\delta p = \Gamma a^2 \delta \rho\) for adiabatic perturbations. However, \[ p' \overset{!}{=} \Gamma a^2 \rho', \tag{10.6}\] instead we have \[ \delta p = \Gamma a^2 \delta \rho \Longrightarrow (p' +\boldsymbol{\xi} \cdot \nabla p_0) = \Gamma a^2 \delta \rho \Longrightarrow p' = \Gamma a^2 \delta \rho + g \rho_0 \xi_z . \tag{10.7}\] The momentum equation reads \[ \rho \frac{\mathrm{d} \mathbf{v}}{\mathrm{d} t} = - \nabla p - g \rho \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \tag{10.8}\] Setting \(\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}'\), \(p = p_0 + p'\), and \(\rho = \rho_0 + \rho'\) yields \[ \rho_0 \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t^2} = - \nabla p_0 - g \rho_0 \hat{\mathbf{z}} - \nabla p' - g \rho' \hat{\mathbf{z}} + \operatorname{HOT} . \tag{10.9}\] The first two terms on the RHS cancel out. Dropping the higher order terms we find \[ \rho_0 \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t^2} = - \Gamma a^2 \nabla \delta \rho + g \frac{\rho_0}{H} \xi_z \hat{\mathbf{z}} - g \rho_0 \nabla \xi_z - g \left( \delta \rho + \frac{\rho_0}{H} \xi_z \right) \hat{\mathbf{z}}, \tag{10.10}\] where we have used Equation 10.7 and \[ \rho' = \delta \rho - (\boldsymbol{\xi} \cdot \nabla) \rho_0 = \delta r + \frac{\rho_0}{H} \xi_z . \tag{10.11}\] The second and last term on the RHS of Equation 10.10 cancel out leaving \[ \rho_0 \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t^2} = - \Gamma a^2 \nabla \delta \rho - g \rho_0 \nabla \xi_z - g \delta \rho \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \tag{10.12}\] Using the fact that \[ \begin{aligned} \nabla \delta \rho &= \nabla (- \rho_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}) \\ &= - \rho_0 \nabla \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}- \nabla \rho_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi} \\ &= - \rho_0 \nabla \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}+ \frac{\rho_0}{H} \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi} \hat{\mathbf{z}}, \end{aligned} \tag{10.13}\] We obtain \[ \begin{aligned} \rho_0 \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t^2} = {}& \Gamma a^2 \rho_0 \nabla \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}- \rho_0 \Gamma g \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi} \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ & - g \rho_0 \nabla \xi_z + g \rho_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi} \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \end{aligned} \tag{10.14}\] This can be simplified to \[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t^2} = {}& c_s^2 \left( \nabla \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}- \frac{a^2}{c_s^2} \frac{1}{H} \nabla \xi_z \right) \\ & - \frac{(c_s^2 - a^2)}{H} \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi} \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \end{aligned} } \tag{10.15}\] We are going to analyze the consequences of this equation shortly. Before we do that, we show that this equation reduces to Eq. (6.31) of Clarke and Carswell (2007) in the limit of vertical oscillations in the \(z\) direction. To do that, we take the divergence of Equation 10.15 to obtain \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial^2 \delta \rho}{\partial t^2} &= c_s^2 \left( \nabla^2 \delta \rho + \frac{\delta \rho}{H^2} \right) + g \rho_0 \nabla^2 \xi_z \\ &\qquad - \frac{(c_s^2 - a^2)}{H} \left( \partial_z \delta \rho + \frac{\delta \rho}{H} \right) \\ &= c_s^2 \nabla^2 \delta \rho - \frac{c_s^2}{H} \partial_z \delta \rho + \frac{a^2}{H} \rho_0 \nabla^2 \xi_z \\ &\qquad + \frac{a^2}{H} \partial_z \delta \rho + a^2 \frac{\delta \rho}{H^2} . \end{aligned} \tag{10.16}\] Assuming that \(\delta \rho = \delta \rho (t, z)\) and \(\boldsymbol{\xi}= \xi_z \hat{\mathbf{z}}\), we obtain, \[ \frac{\partial^2 \delta \rho}{\partial t^2} - c_s^2 \frac{\partial^2 \delta \rho}{\partial z^2} - \frac{c_s^2}{H} \frac{\partial \delta \rho}{\partial z} = 0, \tag{10.17}\] where we have used the fact that \[ \nabla^2 \xi_z = \partial_z^2 \xi_z = \partial_z \left( - \frac{\delta \rho}{\rho_0} \right) = - \frac{\partial_z \delta \rho}{\rho_0} - \frac{\delta \rho}{\rho_0 H} . \tag{10.18}\] Equation 10.17 is identical to Eq. (6.31) of Clarke and Carswell (2007). It describes the propagation of density modes in a stratified atmosphere, but neglects an entirely different class of modes that have \(\boldsymbol{\xi}\) in the z-direction, but propagate in the xy-plane.

As before, we look for traveling wave solutions \[ \boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{\eta}e^{\mathrm{i} (\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x}- \omega t)} . \tag{10.19}\] Inserting this into Equation 10.15 we obtain \[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \omega^2 \boldsymbol{\eta} = {}& c_s^2 \left( \mathbf{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{\eta}+ \mathrm{i} \frac{a^2}{c_s^2} \frac{1}{H} \eta_z \right) \mathbf{k} \\ & + \mathrm{i} \frac{(c_s^2 - a^2)}{H} \mathbf{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{\eta} \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \end{aligned} } \tag{10.20}\]

  • In the limit of \(H \gg a^2 k / \omega^2\), that is, in the limit in which the wavelength of perturbations is much smaller than the scale height of the atmosphere, we recover the dispersion relation for waves in a homogeneous medium, namely \(\omega^2 = c_s k^2\), as expected.

  • Purely horizontal waves, with \(\boldsymbol{\eta}, \mathbf{k} \perp \hat{\mathbf{z}}\) cannot satisfy the dispersion relation, unless \(c_s = a\), that is for isothermal as opposed to adiabatic perturbations. Physically this means that density fluctuations become buoyant, so they acquire a \(k_z \neq 0\) component in their wave vector.

  • Waves with \(\boldsymbol{\eta} \parallel \mathbf{k} \parallel \hat{\mathbf{z}}\) satisfy the dispersion relation simplifies to \[ \omega^2 = c_s^2 \left( k^2 + \mathrm{i} \frac{1}{H} k \right) \Longrightarrow k_{1, 2} = - \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2 H} \pm \sqrt{\left( \frac{\omega}{c_s} \right)^2 - \left( \frac{1}{2 H} \right)^2} . \tag{10.21}\] These are the so called pressure or p-modes of the atmosphere. They are the generalization of sound waves.

    • If \(H \gg \frac{c_s}{\omega}\), then the dispersion relation for p-modes can be approximated as: \[ k_{1, 2} \approx - \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2 H} \pm \frac{\omega}{c_s} . \tag{10.22}\] In this case the displacement grows exponentially with height: \[ \xi_z = \eta_z e^{\mathrm{i} (\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x}- \omega t)} = \eta_z \exp \left[ \mathrm{i} \omega \left( \pm \frac{z}{c_s} - t \right) \right] \exp \left( \frac{z}{2 H} \right) . \tag{10.23}\] This means that waves propagating vertically along an isothermal atmosphere eventually become nonlinear and will experience wave-breaking. Note that the density fluctuation remains finite: \[ \delta \rho = - \rho_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}= - \tilde{\rho} \eta_z \left( \frac{\mathrm{i} \omega}{c_s} + \frac{1}{2 H} \right) \exp \left[ \mathrm{i} \omega \left( \frac{z}{c_s} - t \right) \right] \exp \left( - \frac{z}{2 H} \right) . \tag{10.24}\]

    • If \(H \ll \frac{c_s}{\omega}\), then the solution describes one of two global oscillation modes of the atmosphere: \[ k_{1, 2} \approx - \frac{\mathrm{i}}{H}, 0 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad [\xi_z]_{1, 2} = \eta e^{\mathrm{i} \omega t}, \eta_z e^{z / H} e^{\mathrm{i} \omega t} . \tag{10.25}\]

  • Let us now consider the case of perturbations with horizontal displacement. We can align the \(x\)-axis with \(\boldsymbol{\eta}\), meaning that \(\boldsymbol{\eta}= \eta \hat{\mathbf{x}}\). In this case, the dispersion relation becomes \[ \omega^2 \eta \hat{\mathbf{x}} = c_s^2 (k_x \eta) \mathbf{k}+ \mathrm{i} \frac{(c_s^2 - a^2)}{H} k_x \eta \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \tag{10.26}\] This means that there are two components of \(\mathbf{k}= \left(\begin{array}{ccc} k_x & 0 & k_z \end{array}\right)\). The x-component of Equation 10.26 is \[ \omega^2 = c_s^2 k_x^2 \Longrightarrow k_x = \pm \frac{\omega}{c_s} . \tag{10.27}\] The \(z\)-component of Equation 10.26 is \[ k_z = - \mathrm{i} \left( 1 - \frac{a^2}{c_s^2} \right) \frac{1}{H} . \tag{10.28}\] The corresponding displacement is \[ \boldsymbol{\xi} (t, \mathbf{x}) = \eta \exp \left[ \mathrm{i} \omega \left( \pm \frac{x}{c_s} - t \right) \right] \exp \left[ \left( 1 - \frac{a^2}{c_s^2} \right) \frac{z}{H} \right] \hat{\mathbf{x}} . \tag{10.29}\] Note that, as in the previous case, the density fluctuations \(\delta \rho\) remain finite. These are the so called (internal) gravity-modes or g-modes of the atmosphere. In the limit in which \(c_s \rightarrow a\), that is \(\Gamma \rightarrow 1\) (isothermal perturbations), these type of waves reduce to horizontal sound waves.

10.2 In-class worksheet

We consider small disturbances in a plane parallel, isothermal atmosphere. We will assume \(z\) to be the vertical direction and \(g\) to be the gravitational acceleration, assumed to be constant and towards the negative \(z\) direction. Let \(a = \sqrt{k_B T / \mu}\) be the isothermal sound speed.

  1. Show that the static background density \(\rho_0\) satisfies \[ \rho_0 (z) = \tilde{\rho} e^{- z / H}, \tag{10.30}\] where \(H = a^2 / g\) is the scale height of the atmosphere.

  2. Let \(\delta \rho\) and \(\rho'\) be the Lagrangian and Eulerian perturbations of the density. Show that \[ \rho' = \delta \rho + \frac{\rho_0}{H} \xi_z . \tag{10.31}\]

  3. Let \(p'\) be the Eulerian pressure perturbation. Show that \[ p' = c_s^2 \delta \rho + g \rho_0 \xi_z, \tag{10.32}\] where \(c_s^2 = \Gamma a^2\) is the square of the sound speed and \(\xi_z\) is the \(z\)-component of the displacement.

  4. Use the results from the previous two points to show that \[ \rho_0 \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial t^2} = - c_s^2 \nabla \delta \rho - g \rho_0 \nabla \xi_z - g \delta \rho \hat{\mathbf{z}} . \tag{10.33}\]

10.3 Exercises

Exercise 10.1 (Waves in a polytropic atmosphere) An isentropic atmosphere sits in a uniform gravitational field \(g\), with pressure \(p_0 = A \rho_0^{1 + 1/n}\) and \(A\) constant. Let \(x\) be the depth below the top of the atmosphere, and suppose that the unperturbed density is \(\rho_0 = (kx)^n\) with \(k\) constant.

  1. Show that hydrostatic equilibrium requires \(g = Ak (1 + n)\).

  2. Perturb the atmosphere with a small vertical velocity \(u\), taken positive upward, together with adiabatic perturbations \(p' \ll p_0\) of the pressure and \(\rho' \ll \rho_0\) of the density. Show that \(p' = (gx / n) \rho'\).

  3. Show that the linearized continuity and momentum equations are \[ \frac{\partial \rho'}{\partial t} - u \frac{\partial \rho_0}{\partial x} - \rho_0 \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 0 \tag{10.34}\] and \[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \left( \frac{\rho'}{\rho_0} \right) g - \frac{g}{n \rho_0} \frac{\partial (x \rho')}{\partial x} = 0. \tag{10.35}\]

  4. For \(n = 1/2\), combine these equations to show that \[ \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = 2 gx \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + 3 g \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} . \tag{10.36}\]

  5. Look for solutions \(u (t, x) = u_1 (x) e^{\mathrm{i} \omega t}\) and change variable to \(\tau = (2 x / g)^{1/2}\). Show that Equation 10.36 becomes \[ \frac{1}{\tau} \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d} \tau^2} (\tau u_1) + \omega^2 u_1 = 0. \tag{10.37}\] Find the solution that stays finite at \(x = 0\) and interpret it physically.
